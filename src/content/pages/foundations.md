Corporate psychology — more formally, organizational or industrial-organizational (I-O) psychology — is the applied study of how people think, feel, decide, and behave inside work organizations, and how organizational structures, culture, and leadership shape that behavior in return. It sits at the intersection of psychology and business: the same rigor used to study human cognition and emotion, applied to the specific environment of teams, hierarchies, incentives, and deadlines.

In practice, the discipline covers a wide territory: motivation and engagement, decision-making under pressure, team dynamics, leadership behavior, organizational culture, stress and wellbeing, and how people respond to change. What unites these areas is a basic premise — that performance is never purely a function of skill or strategy. It is also a function of psychological state: whether people feel safe, capable, valued, and clear on why the work matters.

### Why it belongs on the executive agenda, not just in HR

Corporate psychology is often treated as a "soft" concern, delegated to HR or wellbeing programs. The research suggests otherwise. Manager behavior alone accounts for an estimated 70% of the variance in team-level engagement, according to Gallup's global workforce research — meaning the single largest lever on whether a team is engaged or checked out is not compensation or perks, but how directly people are managed day to day. That is a leadership and operations issue as much as an HR one.

Treating corporate psychology as a core operating discipline means measuring it the way you'd measure any other driver of performance: systematically, with real data, and with accountability sitting close to the business rather than off to the side.

### How this shows up across the rest of this site

The topics that follow are not abstract theory — they are the specific psychological pressure points most active in organizations today. Some are longstanding (burnout, trust, change fatigue). One is new and moving fast: the psychological impact of AI adoption, which is reshaping how people relate to competence, security, and their own professional identity in real time.
