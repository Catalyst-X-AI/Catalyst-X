Change fatigue is the cumulative exhaustion that builds when people experience change faster than they can absorb it — not resistance to any single initiative, but depletion from the sheer volume and pace of change over time. McKinsey research has described the current business environment as an "age of perpetual organizational upheaval," and the data on how organizations are coping with it is not encouraging.

Gartner's research frames the stakes plainly: unaddressed change fatigue erodes business performance and directly undermines technology adoption and employee engagement — meaning it isn't a soft, downstream effect but a direct threat to whatever the change was meant to achieve. Peer-reviewed research published in the Journal of Organizational Change Management similarly finds that change fatigue predicts increased strain, burnout, and turnover intent, while negatively affecting both teamwork and job satisfaction.

### What builds resilience to it

Recent academic work offers a genuinely useful lever here: a 2025 study on "self-leadership" found that employees who actively manage their own thoughts, focus, and motivation — rather than relying entirely on the organization to manage change for them — experience significantly less strain from change fatigue, even when the volume of change itself stays constant. This doesn't let organizations off the hook; it points to a dual approach: reduce unnecessary change volume where possible, and deliberately build employees' capacity to self-regulate through the change that remains.
