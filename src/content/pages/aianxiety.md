AI-related job anxiety is not a fringe concern — it is now a mainstream workforce experience, and the trend line is moving quickly. Mercer's Global Talent Trends research, drawing on roughly 12,000 workers and business leaders worldwide, found that 40% of workers now fear AI will make their job obsolete, up from 28% just two years earlier — a 12-percentage-point jump in a very short window.

### Why this fear behaves differently from past automation waves

Earlier waves of workplace automation were understood, broadly, as displacing physical or routine tasks. AI's current wave reaches further into cognitive and knowledge work — writing, analysis, coding, decision-support — the very tasks many professionals treated as reliably "theirs." Psychologically, this produces a distinct flavor of anxiety: not just concern about a role disappearing, but uncertainty intolerance about a much more open-ended question — what does my expertise count for now? That kind of ambiguous, ongoing threat is harder for the mind to resolve than a single, clearly-scoped risk, which is part of why it can persist even in roles that are not, in fact, at meaningful risk of elimination.

### A caution about the freeze response

One of the more revealing findings is behavioral, not attitudinal: the sharp drop in workers actively job-searching is consistent with what psychologists call a freeze response to uncertainty — when a threat feels large and poorly defined, people are less likely to act, not more, even when acting might be in their interest. For organizations, this matters: a workforce that looks calm on the surface may simply be disengaged and waiting, which carries its own long-term risk to retention and initiative.
